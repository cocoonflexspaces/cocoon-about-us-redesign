Origin Story

Space as a matrix.

Eduard Monteagudo grew up on job sites. Surrounded by architects, contractors, and designers, he spent his childhood moving through spaces mid-transformation — watching rooms become something entirely different from what they had been. That experience never left him.

He went on to build a career across every discipline of space design in Barcelona: residential and commercial interiors, experimental design, art installations, music sets, set design, and conceptual work — collaborating with studios like Studio Animal on projects that pushed the boundaries of what designed environments could do and say.

His final thesis at EINA — titled Tinglado — already contained the seeds of what Cocoon would become: how do you make design inclusive instead of exclusive? How do you use it as a tool to build diversified communities around it? Those questions — not a business opportunity — were the origin of everything that followed.

When Eduard moved to New York, he carried those ideas with him. He joined 988Manhattan, an incubator that became the crucible for the next three years — where the conceptual arguments of his thesis were tested, challenged, and eventually shaped into a business model capable of surviving contact with one of the most competitive real estate markets on Earth.

988Manhattan Incubator · 2016–2019

Eduard didn't just work at 988Manhattan — he lived inside it. The incubator was itself a space in constant transformation: a full laboratory that became, over three years, the first iteration of what Cocoon would be. The conceptual arguments of Tinglado were tested here against the hardest real estate market in the world.

In June 2019, Cocoon launched with three properties. Eduard negotiated something unusual with the first property owner: the right to live in the space. Casa Koddous became his home. He experienced firsthand what it meant to be responsible for a space someone else owned — the trust involved, the operational reality, the specific intelligence that only comes from truly inhabiting a place. That understanding is baked into how Cocoon works with every property owner today.

For the three years that followed, Eduard lived nomadically across New York City — moving between neighborhoods, absorbing each one's distinct character and pace of life. Williamsburg to Harlem. The Upper West Side to Carroll Gardens. It was an extended, deliberate education in how people inhabit the same city in profoundly different ways — and what extraordinary spaces in each of those worlds actually look like from the inside.

The Symbol

A cocoon is not a container. It is a transformation in progress.

Eduard has always taken nature as his primary design reference. Not as decoration, but as a structural logic — the way living systems solve problems of form, function, and coexistence at every scale.

The Cocoon mark is inspired by diatoms — microscopic organisms that live at the foundation of Earth's ecosystems. Despite being invisible to the naked eye, diatoms produce roughly a quarter of all the oxygen on the planet. Their glass-like shells are among the most intricate geometric forms in nature: structured, purposeful, extraordinarily beautiful.

They are proof of something Eduard believes deeply: that the smallest, least visible element in a system can be the one everything else depends on.

James Lovelock's Gaia hypothesis describes Earth as a single self-regulating living system — one where every organism, from the diatom to the redwood, plays a necessary role in maintaining the conditions for life. No element is expendable. No participant is merely instrumental.

That is the philosophy behind Cocoon. Every party in the city ecosystem — the property owner, the brand, the creator, the neighborhood — is a dignified node in a network, not a transaction in a chain. When each one is treated that way, the whole system produces something none of them could alone.

01 — Curation

Select with intention.

Every Cocoon property is reviewed by Eduard personally. Architectural merit, interior character, neighborhood story — we look for spaces that have something to say, then amplify it. The result is a portfolio where no two spaces are alike, and every one is worth the trip.

02 — Operations

Manage every event.

A dedicated Casa Manager is on-site for every booking. Owners never have to be involved. Clients never have to worry. From the moment a booking is confirmed to the post-event walkthrough, we carry the responsibility so everyone else can focus on what they came to do.

03 — Design

Design as an ongoing act.

Eduard's design background isn't a credential — it's a lens applied to every property we work with. When the space allows it, we intervene: vegetation, art, furniture, light. The goal, across the entire portfolio, is for every Cocoon space to grow more itself over time, not less.

Where We're Going

Spaces that unlock a city. A city that works for everyone.

Tech, health, and transportation have been radically transformed in the last thirty years. Our cities — the actual physical fabric we inhabit — have barely moved. The reason: real estate is controlled by the most risk-averse investors in the world.

Cocoon changes the equation. By bringing genuine cash flow into the least liquid layer of the city, we create the network — of spaces, relationships, and trust — that makes something far larger possible.

The vision, from Eduard's thesis to today, has never changed: design as a tool to build diversified, inclusive communities. A city where extraordinary spaces belong to the whole ecosystem, not just those who can afford to own them.

Unlock the spaces

We make extraordinary private venues financially viable for their owners — and use that commercial trust to open spaces that self-service platforms cannot access.

Bring nature back into the city

Not biophilic plant walls. A more radical ambition: reintroducing nature as living infrastructure — green corridors and environments that challenge how cities are designed and built at the planning level.

Design for everyone in the network

From established architects and brand CMOs to minority creators with social visions — Cocoon is where ideas meet venues, and the city of the future gets prototyped in the city of today.

The Founder

Eduard Monteagudo

Founder & CEO

Eduard conceived and built Cocoon from zero — no capital, no team, no playbook. Three years at 988Manhattan incubating the model. Seven years proving it works. He closed every booking personally for the first three years and still closes every six-figure activation today.

His path to Cocoon runs through every discipline of space design. Born into a world of job sites and building transformations, Eduard trained as a space designer, interior architect, and systems builder in Barcelona — developing a practice that moved fluidly between residential and commercial interiors, experimental design, art installations, set design, and conceptual work. Studios like Studio Animal, where he collaborated on projects in Barcelona, shaped his conviction that designed environments carry cultural meaning, not just aesthetic value.

His final thesis, Tinglado, argued that design should build inclusive communities — that extraordinary spaces should serve the whole social ecosystem, not only those wealthy enough to own them. Cocoon is that argument, made operational.
